The format

Bharat Niti Lipi, in one page

BNL is Akoma Ntoso 3.0 (OASIS LegalDocML) with two attributes added, a fixed way of using the standard for Indian legal events, and a rulebook. Every BNL file is a valid Akoma Ntoso file, so every tool built for the standard can read it.

Four principles

  1. Valid Akoma Ntoso, always. No fork of the standard.
  2. The standard's own words first. BNL adds only what Indian law has and the standard does not.
  3. One fact, one place. An amendment, a judgment or a delegation is recorded once, against the provision it affects, pointing at the instrument that caused it. A State's text, or the law on a date, is computed and never stored.
  4. Every effect cites its source: a gazette notification, or a judgment down to the paragraph.

What BNL adds

AttributeValuesSays
bnl:extentIN, or State codes such as IN-UP IN-ORWhere a change applies: a State amendment, a High Court's order.
bnl:basisstated · held · inferred · contestedHow a link or effect is known: the instrument says so, a court has held it, an analyst concluded it, or authorities disagree.

Both are typed in bnl.xsd. Akoma Ntoso accepts attributes from other namespaces on its elements, so validate.xsd checks a file against the official schema and BNL together.

The rulebook

The standard says what a legal document may contain; these rules say what an Indian one must (rules.sch, run with any Schematron validator):

  • BNL-1 Every section and rule has a commencement date (a period on it, above it, or on each of its parts).
  • BNL-2 Subordinate legislation states the power it is made under (a legislativeDelegation with basis "stated").
  • BNL-3 Every delegation link says how it is known (bnl:basis).
  • BNL-5 A court's effect on a provision cites the judgment or order.
  • BNL-6 A provision struck down, stayed or read down stays in the text; only its status changes.
  • BNL-7 A stay records when it began (and ends when vacated or decided).
  • BNL-8 Every reference inside a document lands on a provision that exists.
  • BNL-4 A change limited to part of India comes from a State instrument or a court.

Rules that need more than one document are run across the whole set: a rule whose head of power is omitted, a reference into another instrument that lands nowhere, a State amendment without its Article 254(2) assent.
